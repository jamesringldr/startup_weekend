# Q&A Prep — Final Pitch (Sunday)

## The source material (YC interview guidance, transcribed)
YC interviews are 10 minutes. Partners have already read your application. There's no long presentation. They want to understand what you're building, what you've learned, and how much progress you've made.

- **The product.** What are you building? Explain it in 1–2 simple sentences. No buzzwords, no long vision. If they don't understand it, you've lost valuable time.
- **The users.** Who is using it? Where did they come from? What do they use it for? Knowing your users matters more than saying you're targeting millions.
- **The numbers.** Growth, retention, usage, revenue, unit economics. Know what is happening inside your product. If you don't know a number, don't invent one.
- **The competition.** What are existing products getting wrong? Why do customers choose you? Saying your product is better or easier isn't enough. Explain exactly how.
- **The problems.** What's not working? What has surprised you? Where are you struggling? YC specifically values honest answers over pretending everything is going perfectly.
- **The progress.** What have you built since applying? Have you launched, added users, improved the product, or generated revenue? YC says making real progress before the interview is one of the best ways to improve your chances.
- **The demo.** Have a working version ready. They may ask to see what you've built. Don't waste interview time trying to log in, find a link, or get your product running.
- **The founders.** All founders should be on the call. Be ready to explain your work, your decisions, and what you've learned. YC wants to understand the people building the company, not just the product.

These aren't eight official interview questions. They're the areas YC's own interview guidance tells founders to understand.

**Don't prepare a 10-minute pitch. Prepare to answer questions about your business without hiding behind a presentation.**

**Know your users. Know your numbers. Show what you've built.**

## How it maps to THIS weekend (adaptation — read this, not just the YC text)
The weekend's Q&A is **2 minutes after a 3-minute pitch** — not a 10-minute interview. That means ~4–6 questions max, 20 seconds each. Every area below has an owner on the team and a judge it answers. The discipline is the same as YC's: short, specific, honest — and never invent a number.

- **The product → James.** One or two simple sentences, no buzzwords. Answers Fein and Clarke's "what is it, really?" test. Practice line: "An exchange where sports bettors trade positions like stocks — buy in, sell out, exit anytime — instead of being locked into the house's price."
- **The users → Validation lead.** Who you talked to Saturday, where they came from, what they use it for. Knowing your users beats claiming millions. Answers Bergman's validation lens. Bring the tally, not adjectives.
- **The numbers → Numbers person.** Growth, usage, unit economics — at weekend scale, that means: interviews completed, positions placed in the demo, take-rate math, liquidity math. If you don't know a number, say so. Answers Fein/Clarke. Inventing one is how you lose the room.
- **The competition → Numbers person + James.** What DraftKings, Betfair, Kalshi/Polymarket get wrong — exactly how, not "better or easier." The exchange answers: the house is the counterparty and prices your exit; we make the market, participants price each other. Answers Fein and (if he asks) Tasset's investor read.
- **The problems → James.** What's not working, what surprised you, where you're struggling. YC values honest answers — and so does Grace, whose solo-founder advice is literally "be vulnerable about your limitations and say how you're filling the gaps." The honest problem to name if asked: cold-start liquidity — the first bid/ask has no incumbent channel. Name it before a judge does, with the staged answer attached.
- **The progress → Builder + James.** What changed between Friday and Sunday, in numbers. This is Grace's signature concept — **momentum**. "Friday night we had an idea; Saturday we interviewed N bettors and X% said they'd been locked into a position they wanted out of; Sunday we have a working book with live bid/ask and an exit." Progress is the whole pitch in miniature.
- **The demo → Builder.** Working version ready, embedded in the slide as a recorded video (primary) — never waste Q&A time logging in, finding a link, or getting the product running. If a judge asks to see it live, the builder drives; James narrates.
- **The founders → Whole team.** Everyone is on stage, everyone is ready for Q&A. Team Dynamic is scored on exactly this: shared delivery, everyone engaged. Each member owns their topic (see assignments above); nobody answers a question outside their topic unless it's theirs by default (James = regulation + market math; validation lead = user follow-ups; builder = liquidity/demo).

## Q&A bank — the questions to pre-write (20-second answers, owner in brackets)
- **Regulation:** "Isn't this illegal / how do you get licensed?" [James] — Staged path, inside the terrain: start as a free-to-play / paper-trading market to prove mechanics, then state-by-state licensing with a compliance-first design; Betfair's US absence is a regulatory moat, not an oversight. Never frame crypto/USDC as a workaround.
- **Liquidity / cold start:** "Who posts the first bid?" [Builder / James] — Honest answer + the Wagner question reframed: seed market-makers, one league, one contract, narrow the book until it clears. Name the problem; don't hand-wave it.
- **Incumbents:** "Why doesn't DraftKings just do this?" [Numbers / James] — Because the house edge IS their business model; an exchange cannibalizes it. Different business, not a feature.
- **What-ifs (Clarke):** "What if handle drops 50%? What if a regulator pauses you? What if your market-maker exits?" [James] — Pre-compute one-line answers with numbers; the point is showing you've thought in scenarios, not having perfect ones.
- **Validation challenge:** "How many people did you actually talk to?" [Validation lead] — Exact tally, exact method, exact quote. Never round up.
- **Team:** "Who built what?" [Whole team] — Each member states their piece in one sentence. This is the Team Dynamic score being earned live.

## Rules for the 2 minutes
- **20 seconds per answer, then stop.** Long answers eat the next question.
- **Answer the question asked, not the pitch you wish you'd given.**
- **"I don't know yet" beats a made-up number.** Every time. YC says it, Grace says it, Fein grades on it.
- **Never say:** "Billionaire maker," "disrupting gambling," unfooted TAM, or that the exchange is unregulated space.
- **If a judge asks something nobody owns,** James takes it, answers in one sentence, and offers the follow-up after ("happy to walk through the math with you after — the short version is…").
