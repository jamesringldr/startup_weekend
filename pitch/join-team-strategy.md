# Join Team Strategy — Exchange Doesn't Get Picked

James's persona direction: **"the operator who ships"** — same persona as the team-build plan, different seat. You are not selling an idea. You are selling **execution velocity** — the scarcest thing in the room. Most teams Friday night will have idea people and maybe one developer. Almost nobody has a person who can run a multi-agent build pipeline and turn an idea into a working demo by Saturday night.

## The 20-second Friday-night script
> "I'm James — I spent 18 months building a consumer privacy product solo. I don't write every line of code by hand; I run AI coding agents, so I can ship a working demo in a day. I also run teams — I'll organize our build, our validation, and our pitch so nothing falls through the cracks. If you need someone who makes the weekend actually ship, I'm your person."

## What you offer a team (in priority order)
1. **Build lead / technical orchestrator.** You direct Claude Code, Cursor, and Grok to build moderately complex tech fast. Be honest about the method — you orchestrate AI builders, you don't hand-write every line — and pair with their developer if they have one: you make them 3x faster. If they have no developer at all, you ARE the build path.
2. **Product / ops lead.** You run the weekend like a sprint: intake, roles, checkpoints (Sat 10am / 2pm / 6pm, Sun 10am), validation-sprint design, deck structure, pitch rehearsal. This is the Gantt-orchestration skill from your own prep — apply it to their team.
3. **Pitch co-lead.** Plain-spoken presenter; you open or close, and you anchor Q&A on business model and strategy. Your tone (specific, unhurried, honest about unknowns) is a team asset on stage.

## What you do NOT sell
- Pure designer. Pure specialist developer. Domain expert in their field (you aren't one). "Idea guy" — you have ideas, but that's not your value to their team.
- Do not lead with Vanyshr. Do not lead with the exchange. The team you join is the weekend's project; Vanyshr stays Track B.

## How to pick which team to join (in order)
1. **A real shipping gap** — they have an idea and no credible path to a working demo. That's where you're worth the most.
2. **A problem you can understand in an hour** — consumer, healthcare, fintech, data, security. Your workflow intuition and domain history (healthcare IT, privacy, operations) transfer there. Avoid deep-science or hardware ideas where you'd be learning the domain all weekend.
3. **A lead who will let you run ops** — not a control freak. You're a force multiplier, not a passenger; if the lead won't delegate the build and the schedule, you'll be wasted.
4. **A real Sunday shot** — the idea is validation-able and demo-able in 48 hours. If there's no demo path, there's no Execution & Design score, and no placement.
- **Avoid:** teams with no builder AND no sense of scope (you'd be carrying everything); teams that are pure vibes with no demo path; teams that already have a strong PM/ops person (you'd be redundant).

## What joining still banks (even without your idea)
- Full 3-day attendance → **$250 token-build credit** + the right to submit an idea post-weekend for **KU mentorship + Techstars Global Founder Catalyst** (win or lose). Ask Bowen or Eromosele early whether the submission must be the weekend idea or can be any idea — the answer decides whether it can carry Vanyshr.
- Founder Pipeline selection is per-team, so it attaches to the team you join — but a strong weekend on any team still puts you in front of Morris (Pipeline coach) and the judges.
- The Track B game (Fein, Grace, Mitchell, Courtney, Bergman, Peterson, Shackelford via his wife) is **unaffected** by which team you join. Your weekend excellence is the credential either way.
